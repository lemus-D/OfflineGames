# Yes-General

> I want to make a strategy game somewhere between catan and pre civilization
> bronze age which is a little flash game people used to play.

That is the original pitch, and unlike Sharky it has not had a design
conversation yet. This file records what is already settled by the project-wide
rules, plus the one structural decision that must be made before any content is
written.

**Nothing here is built yet.** Read
[../docs/TECHNICAL-DECISIONS.md](../docs/TECHNICAL-DECISIONS.md) first.

---

## What is already settled

Only by inheritance from the project-wide decisions, not by anything specific to
this game:

- Ships as an installable PWA, playable offline (D1).
- No build step, plain ES modules, no runtime dependencies (D2).
- All art generated by code, zero asset files (D3).
- Saves namespaced `yes-general.v1.*`, versioned, keyed by name (D8).
- Deterministic simulation, no `Math.random()` (D9). This matters more here
  than in Sharky: a strategy game wants reproducible maps from a seed, and
  players will want to replay or share one.
- Shared code copied from other games rather than imported (D7).

---

## This game has no size or rendering problem

Worth stating so effort goes to the right place. Hexes, icons, text and panels
land well under 150 KB with no library at all — a fraction of `moon-rover`'s
379 KB, and that includes a 3D engine.

Procedural art is also *easy* here in a way it is not for Sharky. Hex tiles,
resource icons and terrain are geometric and pattern-based, which is exactly what
code-drawn art is good at. There is no charismatic-creature problem to solve.

**The difficulty is entirely design:** economy balance, era progression, and AI
opponents that are interesting to play against. Those are the hard parts, and
none of them are technical.

---

## The one decision that must come first

**The rules and content must be data-driven from the first commit.**

This is not a style preference. `moon-rover`'s own `ARCHITECTURE.md` documents
what the alternative costs, in a game with only five missions:

> A new kind of objective means editing `gameplay.js`. Worse, there are
> comparisons against `missionIdx` (`< 4`, `=== 4`, `>= 3`) that gate content by
> position in the campaign — insert a mission in the middle and they silently
> point at the wrong one. Even "drive 120 m" is a literal in code, not a value
> in the mission data.
>
> Fix this before writing story content, not after. Each mission added before
> the refactor makes the refactor more expensive.

A strategy game is that same trap at ten times the scale. Buildings, resources,
technologies, terrain types, victory conditions and AI behaviours are all
content, and all of them will be edited hundreds of times during balancing.

So: resources, recipes, buildings, techs and their prerequisites live in data
tables, evaluated by a small generic engine. A tech is a row, not a function. If
adding a building requires editing the turn loop, the structure is wrong and it
will only get more expensive.

The balancing pass is the majority of the work on a game like this, and it is
only affordable if changing a number means changing a number.

## Open questions

Nothing below has been decided. These need a design conversation before there is
anything worth building.

- **Scope.** Single-player against AI, or hotseat, or both? Offline-first (D1)
  makes any real-time multiplayer a non-starter, but hotseat is free.
- **Map.** Hex or square? Fixed size, or procedurally generated from a seed?
  Catan implies a small hand-shaped board; Pre-Civilization implies a scenario.
- **Turn structure.** Discrete turns, or the semi-real-time tick that
  Pre-Civilization used?
- **Session length.** This is the question that shapes everything else. A
  twenty-minute game and a two-hour game are different designs, and the answer
  also decides whether saving mid-game is essential or optional.
- **Where the Catan half actually shows up.** Resource trading? Placement and
  adjacency? Dice-driven yields? "Between Catan and Pre-Civilization" is a
  feeling, and it needs to become a mechanic.
- **What the player is doing minute to minute**, and what makes a turn
  interesting enough to take another one.
- **AI opponents.** By far the largest technical risk in this game. A strategy
  game is only as good as the opponent, and a bad AI cannot be balanced around.

---

## Recommendation on order

Build Sharky first. It is smaller, its loop can be proven fun in a few steps,
and it exercises the whole pipeline — PWA packaging, offline saves, procedural
art, the dev server — on a game where the design is already settled.

Yes-General should start from a design conversation, not from code.
